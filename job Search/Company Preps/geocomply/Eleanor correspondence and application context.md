## Smarth's email to Eleanor

Hi Eleanor,

I just saw your post about the Graduate Rotational Program. Having read the posting, I believe the Technology graduate program is an even stronger fit for how I work and where I want to grow, and I’d love to be considered.

Your point about people who build things nobody asked for and make the case for their own next role really stood out to me. In past internships, I’ve proposed improvements and taken responsibility for delivering them. More recently, I’ve gone out and found my own freelance customers, building relationships and identifying where software could help their businesses. That means creating the opportunity, figuring out which problem is worth solving, and following through with working software, often before there’s a clear brief.

I’m particularly interested in Engineering and AI and Automation. Much of my work has involved understanding manual workflows and building tools to improve them. In my own projects, I’ve also built an AI assistant platform that connects everyday services to automate tasks across them. I enjoy learning unfamiliar systems and figuring out where these tools can make a practical difference, while checking that what I build works reliably.

The rotations appeal to me because I want to understand more of the business and take on harder work as I develop. Spending a rotation alongside customers would help me understand their priorities and constraints firsthand, then bring that understanding back into engineering. The longer program would also give me time to see how my decisions hold up as people use what I’ve built.

I’d bring initiative, curiosity, and a willingness to take responsibility wherever I can contribute most. Given that I’m already at the case study stage for STRETCH, could I be considered for the Technology graduate program through the current process? I’m happy to submit a separate application if needed.

Best,

Smarth

## Eleanor's reply

Hey Smarth, great email!

STRETCH and the Rotational Program actually use the same case, which I think you’ve already received. It tests how you work through a messy problem, and we need that skill at every level. What’s different is the bar: for a four-month intern we’re looking for promise, and for an 18-month rotational grad we’re looking for someone we’d trust to own real work from week one.

The rotational program is also much smaller. We run 20–40 internships a year globally, but only three people went through the rotational program in 2026. This term we might have 4–6 STRETCH slots, and it wouldn’t surprise me if we hired just one rotational grad for Jan. Past interns who’ve since graduated get priority too, because we’ve already seen their work.

It would also include a more intensive interview process than STRETCH, including meeting with someone at the director level of the company.

None of that means you’re out of the running! I’ll consider you for both as you go through the case / interviews. I just wanted to be upfront about where each program sits :)

Eleanor
