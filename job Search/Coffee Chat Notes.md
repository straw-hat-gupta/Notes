# Coffee Chat Prep Guide: Jenny Song

## 1. Main goal for the conversation

Your goal is to get company insights about Google’s team matching process, especially how to stand out while you are waiting to be matched. You also want to understand what teams might exist in Waterloo and Montreal, and whether Jenny knows anyone in those offices who would be open to speaking with you.

Keep the conversation focused on learning, not asking for a referral or pushing too hard. Jenny is already doing you a favour by taking the call, so the tone should be curious, appreciative, and respectful.

## 2. What you know about Jenny

Jenny is currently an Associate Product Manager at Google in New York. She previously did an Associate Product Manager internship at Google in the San Francisco Bay Area from May 2022 to August 2022, then started full-time at Google in October 2023.

Before Google, she interned at Microsoft as a Program Manager Intern in Vancouver and also worked as a Business Technical Analyst and Design Co-op at Manulife in Toronto. She went to Western University and was involved with Western Founders Network as VP Industry.

Her path is interesting because she has experience across product management, program management, business/technical analysis, and startup/community involvement.

## 3. Common ground you can bring up

You were introduced through Aradhya, who is a mutual connection.

She works at Google, and you are currently in the Google team matching process.

She has experience moving from internship to full-time at Google, so she may understand the uncertainty and waiting period around conversion, matching, and hiring.

She also has Canadian connections through Western, Vancouver, Toronto, and possibly the Waterloo/Montreal network you are interested in.

## 4. How to open the call

Start with something simple and warm:

“Thanks again for taking the time to speak with me. I really appreciate it. Aradhya mentioned you might be a great person to talk to since you’re at Google and may know more about the different office networks. I’m currently in team matching, so I was hoping to learn from your experience, understand how matching works from the inside, and get a better sense of how I can position myself well.”

Then give her a quick 30-second background:

“I recently graduated from UBC and have been interviewing for software engineering roles. My strongest experience is from a startup where I built a production automation system end-to-end, working across Python, React, embedded systems, sensors, and control hardware. I’m currently in Google team matching, and I’m trying to better understand what teams look for and how candidates can stand out at this stage.”

## 5. Topics to cover

### Topic A: Her background

Start with her. It makes the conversation feel natural and not transactional.

Good angle:  
“I saw that you started as an APM intern and then joined full-time later. I’d love to hear more about your path into Google and product.”

### Topic B: Google team matching

You want to understand how team matching actually works, what hiring managers care about, and how candidates can stand out.

Be careful not to phrase it as “how do I get picked?” Instead, phrase it as “how can I communicate fit clearly?”

### Topic C: Product match / manager match

Your understanding may not be fully accurate, so ask it as a clarification instead of stating it as fact.

Good phrasing:  
“I’ve heard different explanations of team matching, so I’m trying to understand it better. From your experience, how does the matching process usually work once a candidate reaches that stage?”

### Topic D: Waterloo and Montreal offices

This is one of your key goals, but bring it up near the middle or end after building rapport.

Good phrasing:  
“I’m especially interested in understanding what kinds of teams are based in Waterloo or Montreal. Do you know anyone in those offices who might be open to a quick conversation?”

### Topic E: Her transition and gap between internship and full-time

Ask this gently. Avoid “were you stressed?” directly at first. Make it about navigating uncertainty.

Good phrasing:  
“I noticed there was some time between your Google internship and starting full-time. I’d be curious how you navigated that period, especially with the uncertainty around next steps.”

## 7. Questions to prioritize if the call is short

Ask these first:

1. “How does team matching usually work from your understanding?”
    
2. “What do hiring managers look for during matching?”
    
3. “How can I stand out from other candidates at this stage?”

## 8. How to ask about the internship after graduation

Instead of saying:  
“How did you do an internship after graduating? I thought internships required you to go back to school.”

Say:  
“I was curious about your APM internship timeline. I noticed you interned in 2022 and then started full-time in 2023. Was that part of a return offer process, or was there a separate matching period before you joined full-time?”

This sounds more polished and less like you are questioning her path.

## 9. How to ask about stress or uncertainty

Instead of saying:  
“Were you stressed?”

Say:  
“That period between internship and full-time seems like it could be uncertain. How did you handle that waiting period, and was there anything that helped you stay proactive?”

## 10. How to ask for introductions

Near the end, say:

“This has been really helpful. One thing I’m still trying to learn more about is the Waterloo and Montreal offices. Do you happen to know anyone there who might be open to a quick 15-minute conversation? No worries at all if not, but I’d really appreciate any direction.”

## 11. Strong way to close the conversation

“Thank you again, this was genuinely helpful. I really appreciate you taking the time. I’ll keep you posted on how the team matching process goes, and if there’s anyone you think would be useful for me to speak with, I’d be grateful for the connection.”
