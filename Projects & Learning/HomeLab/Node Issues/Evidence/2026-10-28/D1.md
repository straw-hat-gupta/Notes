# D1 dropout evidence — 2026-10-28

[[Projects & Learning/HomeLab/Node Issues/Node Issues|Node Issues]] · [[Projects & Learning/HomeLab/Homelab Setup/Status|Current status]] · [[Projects & Learning/HomeLab/Node Issues/Open Questions|Open questions]]

## Finding

Three retained dropout windows show repeated `e1000e 0000:00:19.0 nic0: Detected Hardware Unit Hang`, Corosync losing its peers, and the local node becoming a non-quorate singleton. Kernel and userspace activity continue after isolation. Physical power-button events precede recovery boots.

Confidence is high that these three incidents involve D1's onboard Ethernet/transmit path. The evidence does not distinguish a defective adapter from driver, firmware, offload, or another network-triggered interaction. It does not establish that every reported dropout had this cause.

## Shutdown and maintenance controls

- All three affected boots record a physical short power-key event after network isolation. Their final journal entries stop during shutdown; `stopall` task records have `unexpected status`. This supports manual recovery with incomplete shutdown evidence, not a proven graceful completion or a spontaneous power failure at dropout onset.
- Older boot endings differ: June 20 ends with a completed node-exporter APT-metrics service; June 25 21:43:12 UTC ends with journald `Under memory pressure, flushing caches.`; the following 41-second boot ends with `pvestatd` reporting `status update time (9.607 seconds)`. `last` labels these three endings `crash`. No NIC unit-hang entries occur in those boots. Missing shutdown records and `last` labels alone do not establish their causes.
- APT history records `apt-get dist-upgrade` on October 28 09:03:20–09:06:01 MDT / 15:03:20–15:06:01 UTC. Relevant changes include PVE manager 9.2.3→9.2.24, kernel metapackage 7.0.2-7→7.0.14-26, Corosync 3.1.10-pve2→3.1.10-pve3, libknet 1.31-pve1→1.35-pve2, and pve-firmware 3.18-3→3.18-8. This postdates the three historical onsets.
- October 28 boots ending 15:07:27.744616 UTC (`01a9730c6edc468fa4035d8d9c377d4f`, mono 65844.008892) and 15:15:23.228527 UTC (`67998c231d13455e9876925bd966150c`, mono 414.096472) record orderly reboot targets, journal stop, and successful `stopall` tasks. The second and current boots both run the newly installed kernel. No unit-hang entries appear in these short boots.
- Normal reboot control: in boot `67998c231d13455e9876925bd966150c`, Corosync reports `Node 1 was shut down by sysadmin` at 15:15:20.838430 UTC / mono 411.706374, then pmxcfs reports lost quorum at 15:15:20.879402 / mono 411.747346. Kernel warning `watchdog: watchdog0: watchdog did not stop!` follows at 15:15:23.070068 / mono 413.938012, inside the reboot sequence. That warning is not evidence of watchdog-initiated fencing.
- All-boot service context includes 19 watchdog-mux software-watchdog startup reports and 17 Corosync `Watchdog not enabled by configuration` reports. This distinction does not prove that every possible HA watchdog mechanism was disabled.
- pmxcfs startup initialization errors also occur in a healthy current boot: `quorum_initialize failed: CS_ERR_LIBRARY (failed to connect to corosync)` at 15:16:33.041271 UTC / mono 8.917352, followed by `node has quorum` at 15:16:39.051494 / mono 14.927575. They are not a unique dropout signature.
- Historical context includes 29 pvestatd LV-activation errors during the first five minutes of the May 8 boot, 91 guest-agent ping timeouts through June 25, and 128 pveproxy restart sequences. Their existence alone does not tie them to the August/September NIC failures.

## Competing explanations

| Explanation | Assessment from retained D1 records |
| --- | --- |
| Adapter/driver/firmware/offload transmit-path failure | Strongest explanation for these three windows: repeated device-specific unit-hang reports, stalled sampled transmit-ring state, peer loss, continued local activity, recurrence across three boots. Exact component/cause remains unisolated. |
| Cable, switch port, or upstream network problem | Still possible as a trigger or contributing fault. KNET path-down messages are not physical carrier-down evidence. No contemporaneous switch counters or cable/port comparison were collected. |
| Entire host hung or lost power at onset | Weakened for these three windows by continuous kernel/userland logging after isolation and later physical power-key handling. Separate June boot endings remain unexplained. |
| Cluster-only or UI-only fault | Weakened by the NIC's device-specific detector reports and repeated all-peer isolation. D1-only logs cannot establish remote reachability for every protocol. |
| Storage/OOM/GPU/power/thermal issue | Must be assessed against the severe-error scan and other health records. A separate fault can coexist; no unsupported exclusion follows from missing logs. |

The two replacement NICs remain uninstalled. No replacement, offload adjustment, driver reload, reboot, or other fix was applied in this investigation. Future confirmation needs the cross-node/Prometheus correlation and a controlled, separately approved change with a long enough observation period for the historical recurrence intervals.
