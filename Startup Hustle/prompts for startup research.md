# Vancouver-First AI Startup Target Research

Act as a senior startup researcher, technical recruiting analyst, and AI industry analyst.

Your task is to identify, verify, and rank technology startups that I should target for an early-career software engineering job.

Do not create a study guide, learning plan, project plan, or interview-preparation schedule. Your only purpose is to produce an evidence-backed startup target list and explain why each company belongs on it.

## 1. Candidate Profile

Use this profile to evaluate company and role fit.

### Background

- Recent University of British Columbia graduate.
- Bachelor of Science in Computer Science and Physics, completed May 2026.
- Canadian citizen authorized to work in Canada.
- Based in Calgary and open to relocating to Vancouver.
- No current United States work authorization.
- Interested in a first long-term, full-time software engineering role.
- Internship, contract, freelance, and substantial project experience should count as relevant experience.

### Technical Experience

My experience includes:

- Python, TypeScript, JavaScript, SQL, React, Django REST Framework, FastAPI, Flask, Node.js, and Express.js.
- PostgreSQL, MySQL, Redis, MongoDB, Celery, Docker, GitHub Actions, Linux, AWS, REST APIs, OAuth 2.0, JWT, and RBAC.
- Production backend services, workflow automation, relational data modeling, authentication, third-party integrations, testing, logging, alerting, and incident response.
- Computer vision, physical-system automation, sensor integration, device controls, and real-time monitoring.
- Production AI and agentic workflows through an existing Agentic Productivity Platform.

Relevant evidence includes:

- Built a React, TypeScript, Python, and FastAPI production automation platform that tripled protocol deployment speed and doubled weekly production yield.
- Implemented structured logging, contextual tracebacks, alerts, execution history, and remote incident-response tooling.
- Improved computer-vision analysis accuracy by 25 percent and increased automated data acquisition by more than 90 percent.
- Built financial and operational platforms using React, Express.js, Node.js, Python, MySQL, REST APIs, RBAC, relational constraints, and audit history.
- Built an agentic productivity platform with 60 tools and actions, seven OAuth integrations, 34 authenticated API routes, Celery scheduling, retries, rate-limit handling, and a 256-test backend suite.

## 2. Target Work

Prioritize companies where I could eventually work on:

- Production AI agents and workflows.
- AI infrastructure and developer platforms.
- Applied AI product development.
- Backend and platform engineering.
- AI-enabled workflow automation.
- Data, search, retrieval, and research products.
- Vertical AI, especially healthcare, biotechnology, cybersecurity, and operational software.

The role itself does not need “AI” in its title.

Any software engineering role at a genuinely AI-native company may qualify. However, rank the following roles highest:

1. Backend Software Engineer.
2. Platform or Infrastructure Engineer.
3. Applied AI or AI Product Engineer.
4. AI Infrastructure or Evaluation Engineer.
5. Backend-heavy Product Engineer.
6. Developer Tools Engineer.
7. Full-Stack Engineer with substantial backend ownership.

Rank frontend-only roles lower and do not treat them as primary matches.

Exclude pure research roles requiring a master's degree, PhD, or substantial publication history.

## 3. Mandatory Company-Stage Gate

The main target pool must contain private technology product companies at Series B or later.

Qualifying stages include:

- Series B.
- Series C.
- Series D.
- Series E or later.
- Late-stage private or pre-IPO technology startups.

Exclude:

- Pre-seed companies.
- Seed-stage companies.
- Series A companies.
- Companies with an unknown or unverifiable stage from the qualified lists.
- Accelerators and incubators as employers.
- Agencies, consultancies, outsourcing firms, and staffing companies.
- Public or acquired companies unless they still operate independently and are placed in a clearly labeled exception section.
- Companies whose only suitable opportunity is a founding-engineer role.

Exclude OnDeck AI because it does not meet the Series B-or-later requirement.

Do not relax the funding-stage requirement to create a longer list.

For every company, verify:

- Most recent known funding round.
- Funding announcement date.
- Whether the company remains private and active.
- Source supporting the funding-stage classification.

If the stage cannot be verified, place the company in an “Unverified Stage” section and do not include it in the rankings.

## 4. Geographic Priority

Use four clearly separated search groups.

### Group 1: Primary Vancouver Targets

Series B-or-later AI-native companies that:

- Are headquartered in Metro Vancouver, or
- Have a meaningful engineering office or team in Metro Vancouver.

This is the highest-priority group.

Do not require an equal number of Vancouver and San Francisco companies. Vancouver should receive greater weight.

### Group 2: San Francisco Targets

Series B-or-later AI-native companies headquartered in or meaningfully based in the San Francisco Bay Area.

Classify each San Francisco company as:

1. Can hire software engineers in Canada.
2. Explicitly supports relevant United States work authorization.
3. High-fit networking target, but hiring or authorization feasibility is unclear.
4. Not currently actionable.

Do not assume sponsorship or TN eligibility. Report only what official sources state.

### Group 3: Vancouver AI-Heavy Fallbacks

Series B-or-later Vancouver technology startups that may not be fully AI-native but have:

- A major AI product line.
- Substantial production AI engineering.
- AI infrastructure or agent development.
- Relevant backend or platform teams supporting AI products.

Keep these separate from the primary AI-native Vancouver list.

### Group 4: Canada-Remote AI-Native Fallbacks

Series B-or-later AI-native companies based elsewhere that explicitly hire software engineers remotely in Canada.

Keep these separate from Vancouver-based companies.

Do not allow fallback companies to inflate the size of the primary Vancouver list.

## 5. Definitions

### AI-Native Company

Classify a company as AI-native only when AI or machine learning is fundamental to:

- The core product.
- The main customer value proposition.
- The company’s technical differentiation.
- The company’s production infrastructure or workflows.

Do not call a company AI-native merely because its marketing mentions AI.

### AI-Heavy Company

An AI-heavy company may have a broader core product but must have a significant AI product line, AI engineering team, or AI-based customer workflow.

Clearly distinguish AI-native companies from AI-heavy companies.

### Meaningful Engineering Presence

A location qualifies only when reliable evidence shows:

- An engineering office.
- Engineering employees based there.
- Current engineering roles tied to that location.
- An established remote-hiring arrangement covering that location.

A sales office or generic remote designation is not enough.

## 6. Early-Career Eligibility

A live role is eligible when at least one condition is verified:

- The title explicitly says new graduate, early career, entry level, junior, associate, Engineer I, SWE I, or an equivalent label.
- No minimum professional experience is stated.
- The role accepts 0 to 2 years of experience.
- The role counts internships, academic projects, research, freelance work, contract work, or equivalent experience.
- The company has an official apprenticeship, residency, rotational program, or recurring early-career program open to recent graduates.

Exclude roles that clearly require more than two years of professional experience unless the role is explicitly designated as new graduate or early career.

Do not treat an internship as an eligible opportunity unless recent graduates are allowed to apply.

Classify each company:

### Apply Now

A live, relevant, early-career-accessible opportunity is verified and the location appears actionable.

### Active Outreach

No immediately eligible position is open, but the company has official or recent evidence of hiring early-career engineers.

### Network and Monitor

The company is a strong technical fit, but no current or recent early-career route is verified.

### Not Currently Actionable

The company is technically relevant but has a clear eligibility, seniority, location, or work-authorization blocker.

Never present historical hiring evidence as a current opening.

## 7. Research Method

Use current web research and state the research date.

Screen a broad initial pool before selecting the strongest companies. Preferably evaluate at least 25 plausible candidates, but do not force a fixed number when the local market is smaller.

Prioritize these sources:

1. Official company websites.
2. Official career pages.
3. Official Ashby, Greenhouse, Lever, Work at a Startup, or company-hosted job descriptions.
4. Official engineering blogs and documentation.
5. Official GitHub organizations.
6. Official funding announcements.
7. Y Combinator company pages when applicable.
8. Credible technology publications and funding databases when primary funding information is unavailable.

For every shortlisted company:

- Verify that it is still active.
- Verify its latest funding stage.
- Verify its Vancouver, San Francisco, or Canada-remote presence.
- Open every current job link and confirm it remains active.
- Record posting or update dates when available.
- Write “not stated” when a date or requirement is unavailable.
- Distinguish verified facts from reasonable inferences.
- Include direct links to the supporting evidence.
- Do not infer technologies from generic job-board tags.
- Do not infer sponsorship, work authorization, remote eligibility, or future hiring.
- Label unresolved information as unknown.
- Prefer official job descriptions over third-party job aggregators.

## 8. Company Scoring

- Work-authorization problems.
- An unverified funding stage.
- Seniority mismatches.
- A lack of engineering presence in the stated location.
- An engineering organization that appears too small to provide meaningful mentorship.
